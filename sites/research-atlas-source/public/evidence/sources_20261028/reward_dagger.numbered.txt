S10:Z003 | Frontier Transfer.We explore whether configurations that perform well on one task remain effective on other tasks. For
S10:Z004 | each source task, we construct its task-specific Pareto frontier using 200 policy rollouts from a diffusion policy trained
S10:Z005 | exclusively on that task. We then apply the configurations on this frontier to each of the other three tasks and retain the
S10:Z006 | Pareto-optimal points to form a transferred frontier.
S10:Z007 |    To measure how closely each transferred frontier matches the target task’s native frontier, we compute the hypervolume
S10:Z008 | ratio (HVR). Specifically, we divide the area under the transferred frontier by the area under the native frontier. The trigger-
S10:Z009 | time range extends from zero to the latest non-dominated point reached by either frontier. An HVR near one indicates that
S10:Z010 | the transferred configurations retain most of the accuracy–latency tradeoff obtained by task-specific tuning.
S10:Z011 |    The results for each source-target task pair are shown in Figure6. Across the 12 ordered source-target pairs, Reward-
S10:Z012 | DAgger achieves a mean HVR of0.92and a median HVR of0.93, indicating thathigh-performing regions of the gate
S10:Z013 | configuration space are largely shared across tasks.
