# StackTwo research review and ranked hypothesis ledger

Research frozen before the fresh training launch, 4 October 2026 (Europe/Berlin). The separately requested overnight run is a single fresh C training seed, not a proposed method improvement. Its outcome belongs in TRAINING_RESULT.md and must not revise this conclusion.

## Conclusion supported by the project

The specific Direct configuration reliably outperforms the specific unnormalized diffusion configuration on this dataset and these fixed starts across three training seeds. The comparison does not isolate architecture, learning objective, sampling stochasticity, or training maturity. Observation normalization improves several diffusion diagnostics in the existing seed, but has not produced a robust complete-stack policy. Expert-state continuations identify both a transport deficit and a release/stability deficit. They weaken an exclusively early-error explanation without identifying one cause of the full gap.

Evidence classes used below: **P** = project measurement; **L** = general primary-literature claim; **H** = hypothesis or proposed intervention. H is not an observed improvement. Ranking reflects current diagnostic priority and discriminability, not calibrated causal probabilities.

## Verification and actual experimental scope

The fresh read-only verification is [existing_evidence_verification.json](existing_evidence_verification.json), reproduced by [audit_existing.py](audit_existing.py). It checked the HDF5 SHA-256, disjoint 180/20 masks, 27,322 training frames, all 381 sealed causal-evaluation files, six normalization-arm checkpoint hashes, the six original model success counts, all 90 exact phase-start states/histories, all 90 stability tails, 9,816 finite phase actions, and 2,052 executed diffusion chunks. No historical training or rollouts were rerun. Existing summaries of the much larger inference study are cited as historical audited evidence; this review did not independently regenerate its 322,560 chunks.

The task is the project's modified simulated StackTwo with two 4-cm cubes and a programmed, stateful StackExpert. It is not an official human-demonstration robomimic Stack score. Policies consume two frames of 15 state features: absolute cube positions, EEF position/quaternion, and finger positions. Object orientation, contact, and expert phase are absent. RGB is unused, so the known black-image demos do not justify excluding these state demonstrations. The expert carries internal phase, target, and dwell-time memory; two observed frames are not proven sufficient to reconstruct every hidden expert variable. [Project pipeline audit](../stack_causal_diagnosis_20261003/DIAGNOSE.md).

Both models predict 16×7 delta-pose/gripper commands. Direct has 67,467,376 parameters; diffusion has 65,648,903. The nominal budget is 30,000 optimizer updates, batch 8, AdamW 1e-4, 500 warmup updates, cosine schedule, and EMA power 0.75. Direct draws windows with replacement; diffusion restarts a shuffled DataLoader each epoch. Equal updates and similar parameter counts do not imply equal FLOPs or equally mature optimization. Direct action-MSE and diffusion epsilon-MSE are different targets, so their raw losses are not a common quality scale. [Main synthesis](../stack_replication_20261001/FINAL_EXPERIMENT_SYNTHESIS.md), [normalization trainer](../stack_normalization_20261001/scripts/train.py).

Training actions at anchor t run from t−1 to t+14. Baseline execution uses chunk indices [1:3], corresponding to t and t+1; index zero is historical. The saved C config has action_horizon=8, while the established evaluator explicitly uses 2. The fresh run preserves this training config and documents the evaluation override. Primary success requires native stack success and 40 additional successful policy steps at 20 Hz. Contact and lack of grasp in the tail are independently checked; partial phases never replace full success. [Evaluator](../stack_normalization_20261001/scripts/evaluate.py).

## Primary-literature review (L) and relevance to this project (H)

**Diffusion Policy — Chi et al., RSS 2023; extended manuscript arXiv:2303.04137v5, 2024.** The method learns observation-conditioned action sequences with a denoising objective and executes a receding horizon. The extended paper discusses position-control benefits, temporal consistency, action normalization and clipping (Sections 4.2–4.3, Appendix A.1). Its evaluation uses checkpoint averaging/best-checkpoint reporting rather than this project's fixed final-checkpoint rule. Section 5.2 also corrects a robomimic evaluation bug: 22 initializations were actually used despite the declared 50. Consequently, original benchmark values, training epochs and success criteria cannot be transplanted here. H: action representation, conditioning and sequence execution deserve controlled tests; the paper cannot diagnose this particular failure. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04137v5), [RSS record](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p026.html).

**ACT — Zhao et al., RSS 2023.** ACT combines a transformer/CVAE sequence model with temporal ensembling of overlapping predictions (Sections IV-A–C and VI). H: it motivates examining inter-chunk continuity. Both local models already chunk actions; adding “chunking” is therefore not a new treatment. Averaging overlapping diffusion samples could blend incompatible modes, and averaging the gripper near zero changes a sign-based controller's command. A full ACT reproduction would change architecture, objective and execution jointly; it is larger than a minimal causal diagnostic. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13705v1), [RSS record](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.html).

**DAgger — Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, AISTATS 2011.** The algorithm aggregates expert labels on states induced by the learner, addressing the difference between demonstration and rollout distributions; its reduction has assumptions about online learning and expert supervision (Algorithm 3 and theoretical analysis). H: targeted recovery labels are relevant if measured distribution mismatch contributes to failure. This requires valid expert labels at learner states and additional simulator/expert interactions. Reinitializing this project's stateful expert on an arbitrary state is not automatically a valid labeler. Existing expert-state failures mean DAgger is not yet a complete explanation or guaranteed remedy. [Original paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf).

**DART — Laskey et al., CoRL 2017.** Noise is injected into executed supervisor actions during demonstration collection, with its level fitted to approximate learner errors; expert corrections become part of the collected data (algorithm and Sections 4–5). H: it can supply recovery coverage. Offline observation jitter with unchanged action labels is a different intervention, especially at contact/phase boundaries. The local randomizer's uniform positive noise path cannot silently be described as centered Gaussian noise. Neither augmentation alone nor DART's grasping benchmark establishes a StackTwo benefit. [Original paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a/laskey17a.pdf), [local augmentation discussion](../../next_research_direction.md).

**Stable-BC — Mehta et al., inspected arXiv:2408.06246v1, 2024.** Sections IV-B/C regularize local error dynamics. The model-free environment formulation still uses robot dynamics and assumes bounded environment-state error; it provides a weaker guarantee than the full model-based case. H: stability regularization could address drift, but contact switches, partial observations and queued chunks complicate applying its assumptions here. A smooth policy or a finite-difference Lift contraction penalty is not an implementation of the original loss, nor a guarantee of retaining this task's native stack success. This review bases technical claims on the inspected preprint, not an uninspected journal revision. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06246v1).

**DBC — Chen et al., ICML 2024.** A joint expert-distribution diffusion model augments a separate policy's conditional BC objective. H: distribution-aware regularization is an option after diagnosis; this is distinct from the project's diffusion action generator. It adds a model and loss rather than isolating the current generator's failure, and its reported navigation/manipulation results are not local evidence. [Primary proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chen24as.html).

**Consistency Policy — Prasad et al., RSS 2024.** Distills pretrained diffusion policies into fewer inference steps, targeting inference speed with competitive task performance in its experiments. H: relevant if latency becomes the limiting factor. The present simulations advance synchronously, and denoising sweeps did not rescue the unnormalized policies; a speedup alone is not evidence that transport or release will improve. [Primary proceedings](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p071.html).

**MimicGen — Mandlekar et al., CoRL 2023.** Generates additional demonstration trajectories in simulation from a small set of source demonstrations across scene changes. H: useful for coverage expansion, but involves task/segment integration and new generated trajectories. It is larger than the requested fixed-data repeat or the next causal intervention. [Primary proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/mandlekar23a.html).

The [authors' diffusion implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/main/diffusion_policy/policy/diffusion_unet_lowdim_policy.py) was also inspected for global conditioning, noise target and observation-horizon alignment. It supports the To−1 execution convention. Differences in its training defaults are possible experiment axes, not proof that this local implementation is defective. The project's default-UNet config-forwarding fix preserves the current architecture/initial weights and does not explain existing outcomes. [Local parity verification](../stack_causal_diagnosis_20261003/unet_config_fix_verified.json).

| 5 | Observation/action representation hides variables needed for precise transitions | Absolute positions, two-frame history, omitted object orientations and expert memory; C normalization changes diagnostics | Direct succeeds with the same representation; window, queue and inversion audits pass; no quaternion sign flips in the dataset | Matched relative-state or controller-target representation with replay parity and several training seeds |
| 6 | Epsilon objective, padding/phase weighting or optimization maturity undertrain critical decisions | Unmasked padded 16-step supervision, constant rotation targets, narrow transitions; low denoising loss coexists with poor task completion | Gaussian epsilon has equal per-channel variance; large raw gripper variance does not imply loss domination. Balancing and dynamics features did not solve the task | Separate fixed-noise, phase/timestep/channel x0-error diagnostics first; then change one objective/weighting factor with replicated training |
| 7 | Observation normalization alone, alignment bug, or insufficient denoising steps fully explains failure | Normalization affects outputs; sampler/configuration errors are technically possible | C still 2/40; B nearly identity; exact 58-window audits and native chunk checks pass; tested DDIM changes still zero on old weights | A newly detected reproducible mismatch would reopen the implementation hypothesis; current evidence does not support it |

The ledger does not equate multimodal sample dispersion with inappropriate expert multimodality, classify every grasp loss as a failure, infer causality from pooled correlations across model classes, or infer a convergence deficit from a small loss. Different hypotheses can coexist.

## Minimal next discriminating experiment (recommendation only)

Run one **fixed-weight C gripper intervention**, using existing final C/seed-1 EMA weights, DDIM-10 and execute-2. Compare nominal control with a single override: if B is natively held, B height is above 0.88 m, and B−A XY separation is greater than 0.03 m, replace a negative gripper command with +1; otherwise use the sampled action unchanged. Keep all six arm commands unchanged. Opening in the target region remains possible. This uses privileged simulator grasp/geometry information as a causal diagnostic, not a deployable learned improvement.

Use the same 40 fixed starts, initial-state hashes and start-specific policy noise seeds for both arms; replay the nominal control to verify reproducibility. Record each override, its pre-action geometry, original/executed gripper action, all grasp losses and release geometry, held positioning, lowering/release, native stack and the full 40-step stability tail. When the trajectories diverge, state equality is no longer expected; common random seeds control initial noise, not later states. Five existing expert-lift anchors can be a secondary localization panel using the same exact-state/history restore, with initial geometry explicitly marked. Do not pool them into the 40-start rate.

The primary paired outcome is the change in complete stable stacks; report all gained/lost start IDs and partial metrics separately. Fewer premature losses plus better held placement would support a gripper contribution. Fewer losses without more stable stacks would identify a partial mechanism, not rescue the task. No effect with frequent valid overrides weakens this specific opening hypothesis; no effect with almost no overrides gives an underpowered intervention and does not rule out other gripper/timing faults. Persistent failures before release support the arm-control hypothesis; persistent tail failures retain the distinct stabilization question. No evaluation-based checkpoint/threshold tuning, significance claim, or automatic follow-on sweep.

This is smaller and more discriminating than training ACT/DBC, collecting DAgger data, changing action representation, or adding several regularizers at once. The existing unnormalized opening-lock experiment remained at 0/20, so it is counterevidence to a universal gripper-only explanation; testing the separately normalized C weights answers a narrower question. The intervention has not been launched by this research task.

## Separate overnight training evidence

The requested fresh job uses C/seed-1 config as its basis with **new training seed 2**, unchanged dataset/masks and learning settings. Its only configuration changes are seed, experiment name and fresh output directory; the copied historical trainer retains normalization checks and the scheduler correction. Atomic checkpoint writes, per-update loss CSV, update/EMA/scheduler audits, source snapshots/hashes and a Windows process-tree watchdog are runtime safeguards, not learning-method changes. The watchdog requests a safe batch-boundary stop before the deadline, hard-stops at 54,000 seconds, evaluates only final weights if time remains, releases its awake request, and exits. It never retries. [Prepared configuration/protocol](prepared.json), [supervision self-test](supervision_self_test.json), [runner](runner.py).

Regardless of success, one fresh seed cannot establish a robust training improvement or explain the Direct-versus-diffusion gap. At most it adds an independently initialized observation-normalized run under the specified budget. The main conclusion above is fixed before launch.
